# P10 — Assume emergence, engineer containment | **12/100**

> Review date: 2026-05-08. Manifesto: arnaudgelas/agentic-engineering-manifesto@08123c263794b5cc24dbb3e1a5897a74288026e9. Framework: OpenSpec 1.3.1. Organisation: Allianz. Industry: European insurance and financial services — SR 11-7, DORA, EU AI Act, GDPR, Solvency II.

## What OpenSpec asserts about this principle

OpenSpec asserts nothing about containment, emergence, security, threat modelling, rate limiting, circuit breakers, or chaos testing. The framework's stated philosophy in `README.md` is "fluid not rigid / iterative not waterfall / easy not complex / built for brownfield not just greenfield / scalable from personal projects to enterprises". Its source-of-truth artefacts are change folders (`openspec/changes/<id>/`) and merged specs (`openspec/specs/<id>/spec.md`) per `docs/concepts.md`, both of which are Markdown documents validated for shape by `openspec validate` (`openspec/specs/cli-validate/spec.md`). The only security-adjacent artefact in the corpus is `openspec/specs/telemetry/spec.md`, which prescribes anonymous PostHog usage telemetry with `OPENSPEC_TELEMETRY=0` / `DO_NOT_TRACK=1` opt-out and which "SHALL NOT include command arguments, file paths, project names, spec content, error messages, or IP addresses". `docs/customization.md` line 326 mentions "security, performance, and testing considerations" only as a sample reviewer-checklist topic to be filled in by the user. No file in `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/`, `OpenSpec/docs/`, `OpenSpec/AGENTS.md`, or `OpenSpec/README.md` defines guardrails, rate limits, circuit breakers, safe fallbacks, prompt-injection mitigation, egress controls, tool-manifest signing, sandboxing, agent identity, or anomaly detection.

## What works

- `openspec/specs/telemetry/spec.md` Requirement "Anonymous data collection" enforces `SHALL NOT include command arguments, file paths, project names, spec content, error messages, or IP addresses` and Requirement "Environment variable opt-out" honours `OPENSPEC_TELEMETRY=0` and `DO_NOT_TRACK=1`, providing a narrow data-minimisation control on the framework's only outbound channel.
- `openspec/specs/telemetry/spec.md` Requirement "CI environment auto-disable" forces telemetry off when `CI=true`, removing one exfiltration path during automated builds.
- `openspec/specs/cli-validate/spec.md` (Requirement: Bulk and filtered validation) provides a non-zero exit on malformed spec/change Markdown via `openspec validate --all --strict --json`, which catches one structural failure mode (corrupted spec deltas) before archive into `openspec/specs/`.
- `openspec/specs/cli-archive/spec.md` defines an archive-time merge that requires the change folder to be syntactically well-formed before deltas are folded into the source-of-truth `openspec/specs/`, providing a thin authoring-time integrity gate.
- `openspec/specs/context-injection/spec.md` and `openspec/specs/rules-injection/spec.md` wrap injected text in `<context>...</context>` and `<rules>...</rules>` tags per `docs/opsx.md` lines 116–125, giving a syntactic delimiter between framework-supplied text and user prompts (a delimiter, not an enforced trust boundary).
- `openspec/specs/legacy-cleanup/spec.md` and `docs/migration-guide.md` lines 60–123 preserve user-authored `openspec/project.md` rather than deleting it, reducing the blast radius of an `openspec update` run on a brownfield repository.

## Where it fails the manifesto's bar

- "Build guardrails, rate limits, circuit breakers, and safe fallbacks before you need them." (`manifesto-principles.md` §10). OpenSpec defines none of these. No rule, command, or schema in `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/` or `OpenSpec/docs/` names a rate limit, circuit breaker, kill switch, or fallback path. This is absent and exposes Allianz to EU AI Act Art. 15(4) (cybersecurity, resilience to errors, faults, inconsistencies) and DORA Art. 9(2) (ICT protection and prevention) for any high-risk insurance use case where OpenSpec sits in the change-management loop.
- "If you have not threat-modeled prompt injection, privilege escalation, and exfiltration vectors for your specific agent topology, you are not security-tested." (`manifesto-principles.md` §10). `OpenSpec/AGENTS.md` is empty (0 lines) and no spec under `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/` mandates a threat model. The `<context>` and `<rules>` tags in `openspec/specs/context-injection/spec.md` are syntactic markers, not an enforced input boundary between external content and internal instruction, so prompt injection is structurally enabled. This is absent and breaches EU AI Act Art. 15 (robustness/cybersecurity) and DORA Art. 25 (testing of ICT tools and systems including TLPT for significant entities).
- "Treat every retrieval artifact, tool response, and agent-to-agent message as untrusted input." (`manifesto-principles.md` §10). `openspec/specs/context-injection/spec.md` injects `openspec/config.yaml` `context:` text directly into prompts and `openspec/specs/rules-injection/spec.md` injects rule text without integrity verification, signing, or schema validation of the injected content as untrusted. This is convention-not-enforcement and exposes Allianz to GDPR Art. 32 (security of processing) and Solvency II Art. 41 (system of governance — sound and prudent management of operational risk).
- "Pinning tools to verified manifests — checksum or signing verification against a known-good registry." (`manifesto-principles.md` §10, supply-chain bullet). `openspec/specs/ai-tool-paths/spec.md` enumerates `AI_TOOLS` entries (`claude`, `cursor`, `windsurf`, `kimi`) by string identifier and writes skills into tool-specific directories without checksum or signing verification of the generated artefacts or of the consuming tool surface. This is absent and breaches DORA Art. 28 (ICT third-party risk) and EU AI Act Art. 15(5) (measures against attacks attempting to manipulate the system through adversarial inputs).
- "Egress controls on tool outputs — agent outputs that include retrieved or generated content must be logged with full trace before leaving the trust boundary." (`manifesto-principles.md` §10, exfiltration bullet). `openspec/specs/telemetry/spec.md` is the only egress channel governed by the framework, and even it is silent on outputs produced by an agent acting through OpenSpec (e.g. spec text written by an LLM into `openspec/changes/<id>/proposal.md`). No spec requires logging of agent-authored content prior to commit. This is scope-partial and breaches GDPR Art. 5(1)(f) (integrity and confidentiality) and DORA Art. 9(4)(d) (mechanisms to detect anomalous activities).
- "If you have not tested with tool outages, noisy retrieval, and adversarial inputs, you are not chaos-tested." (`manifesto-principles.md` §10). No artefact in `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/`, `OpenSpec/test/`, or `OpenSpec/docs/` defines chaos tests, fault-injection runs, or adversarial-input regression suites. `openspec/specs/cli-validate/spec.md` validates Markdown shape only. This is absent and breaches DORA Art. 25–26 (advanced testing of ICT tools, threat-led penetration testing) and ISO/IEC 23894 risk-management process for the AI lifecycle.
- "Evidence laundering — an agent assembles an evidence bundle from outputs it generated, creating circular self-attestation with no independent verification." (`manifesto-principles.md` §10, governance failure modes). `openspec/specs/opsx-verify-skill/spec.md` runs Completeness/Correctness/Coherence checks executed by the same agent class that authored the change; `docs/commands.md` line 336 states verify output "Does not block archive". No requirement bars the authoring agent from producing the verification artefact. This is structurally enabled and breaches SR 11-7 §IV (effective challenge by a party not responsible for development) and EU AI Act Art. 14(4)(c) (human oversight able to disregard, override, or reverse the system output).
- "Waiver accumulation — waivers granted for specific circumstances persist beyond those circumstances, silently expanding the system's effective policy boundary." (`manifesto-principles.md` §10). OpenSpec has no waiver register, no expiry mechanism, and no detection of accumulated waivers across change folders. `openspec/specs/cli-archive/spec.md` does not require waiver-state evidence at archive time. This is absent and breaches `operational-templates/ai-risk-register.md` (waiver expiry) and DORA Art. 6(8) (review of ICT risk-management framework on documented basis).
