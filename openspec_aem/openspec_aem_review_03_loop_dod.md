## Part 4 — Agentic Loop Phase Analysis

OpenSpec is a brownfield specification-and-change-management tool whose own documentation rejects phase gating: `OpenSpec/docs/workflows.md` states "**Actions, not phases** - Commands are things you can do, not stages you're stuck in" and "**Dependencies are enablers** - They show what's possible, not what's required next". The framework's own phase-level construct is the artefact dependency graph in `OpenSpec/docs/concepts.md` — `proposal → specs → design → tasks → implement` — with a single advisory `/opsx:verify` step before `openspec archive`. Mapped to the manifesto's nine-phase loop, the relationship is a **partial structural mismatch**: OpenSpec covers Specify (proposal+specs), Design (design.md), and Plan (tasks.md) reasonably, partially covers Verify (advisory `/opsx:verify`), and has **no counterpart** for Execute as a bounded-autonomy concept, Validate (separated from Verify), Observe (runtime/governance-state observability), Learn (memory/knowledge updates), or Govern (policy enforcement, accountability sign-off, economics review). Telemetry (`OpenSpec/openspec/specs/telemetry/spec.md`) records only the command name and a version string, with no link to specifications, runs, or outcomes.

### Specify | **58/100**

**What OpenSpec does.** OpenSpec treats specifications as Markdown artefacts living in `openspec/specs/<id>/spec.md` with structured `### Requirement:` and `#### Scenario:` blocks in Given/When/Then form. `OpenSpec/docs/concepts.md` (lines 269) states: "A spec is a **behavior contract**, not an implementation plan." The change flow uses `openspec/changes/<id>/proposal.md` for "Why and what" with `## Why` and `## What Changes` mandatory sections (`OpenSpec/openspec/specs/cli-validate/spec.md`, Requirement: "Validation SHALL provide actionable remediation steps", Scenario: "Missing required sections": "For Change: `## Why`, `## What Changes`"). Delta specs use `## ADDED Requirements`, `## MODIFIED Requirements`, `## REMOVED Requirements`, `## RENAMED Requirements`. The `/opsx:onboard` skill (`OpenSpec/openspec/specs/opsx-onboard-skill/spec.md`) drafts proposals interactively. Validation is structural only: `openspec validate` enforces presence of headers, scenario shape, and Zod-schema correctness; it does not enforce business-need validation, measurable value criteria, named accountable humans, blast-radius assessment, or out-of-scope statements.

**What the manifesto requires.** `manifesto.md` defines: "**Specify** defines what to build and why." It further mandates a nine-condition loop-readiness gate: "A specification is **loop-ready** when all of the following are true: **Business need validated.** ... **Value measurable.** ... **Acceptance criteria expressible.** ... **Constraints identified.** ... **Accountable human named.** ... **Blast radius assessed.** ... **Out-of-scope explicitly stated.** ... *Minimum bar: If you cannot answer 'what does business success look like and how will you measure it?' before entering Specify, the loop is not ready to run.*" (`manifesto.md` "What Must Be True Before Entering Specify"). `integration/loop-readiness-for-agent-opportunities.md` extends this with the requirement that agent-surfaced opportunities pass the same gate before consuming engineering capacity.

**The gap.** OpenSpec validates Markdown shape, not loop-readiness. Nothing in `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/cli-validate/spec.md` checks for a measurable success criterion, a named accountable human, a blast-radius assessment, or an out-of-scope statement. `## What Changes` is required as a header but its substantive content is unverified. Under EU AI Act Art. 9 (risk management system) and Solvency II Art. 124 (validation standards), Allianz cannot demonstrate loop-readiness from the OpenSpec proposal alone; under DORA Art. 8 (ICT risk management framework) the missing blast-radius / consequence-class assessment is a control deficiency. Severity: **Medium**.

### Design | **55/100**

**What OpenSpec does.** Design lives in `openspec/changes/<id>/design.md`. `OpenSpec/docs/concepts.md` (Artifact Types — Design) prescribes content: "The design captures **technical approach** and **architecture decisions**", with worked examples covering "Decision: Context over Redux", "Decision: CSS Custom Properties", a "Data Flow" section, and a "File Changes" list. `design.md` is required only when the schema lists it (`OpenSpec/docs/concepts.md` Schemas section: "Dependencies are enablers, not gates. They show what's possible to create, not what you must create next. You can skip design if you don't need it."). `/opsx:verify` "Coherence Verification" (`OpenSpec/openspec/specs/opsx-verify-skill/spec.md`, Requirement: "Coherence Verification") extracts decisions from `design.md` and checks "implementation follows those decisions"; if it diverges the skill emits a WARNING, not a CRITICAL. The default `core` profile (`OpenSpec/docs/workflows.md`) routes `proposal → apply → sync → archive`, omitting `design.md` entirely.

**What the manifesto requires.** `manifesto.md` defines: "**Design** architects how to build it: boundaries, topology, constraints, and coordination rules." `manifesto.md` also requires that "any architectural decisions triggered by governance are filed back into Design" as part of Govern completion conditions, establishing Design as a durable, governance-coupled artefact rather than an optional document.

**The gap.** OpenSpec design is optional, free-form, and has no required content for boundaries, topology, constraints, coordination rules, or defence-in-depth properties. There is no machine-checkable schema for `design.md`; `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/cli-validate/spec.md` does not enforce any section presence inside `design.md`. There is no feedback path that files a Govern-triggered architectural decision back into `design.md`. Under DORA Art. 9 (ICT systems, protocols and tools) and Solvency II Art. 41 (system of governance — written policies on risk management, internal control, internal audit), the absence of mandatory architectural-decision content fails to evidence "boundaries and constraints" for Allianz internal-model agents. Severity: **High**.

### Plan | **62/100**

**What OpenSpec does.** Planning is captured in `openspec/changes/<id>/tasks.md` as a hierarchical Markdown checklist. `OpenSpec/docs/concepts.md` (Tasks artefact section) prescribes: "Tasks are the **implementation checklist** — concrete steps with checkboxes" with the worked example showing groups, hierarchical numbering (`1.1`, `1.2`), and `- [ ]` / `- [x]` markers. Verify reads tasks.md and "counts tasks marked `- [x]` (complete) vs `- [ ]` (incomplete)" (`OpenSpec/openspec/specs/opsx-verify-skill/spec.md`, Requirement: "Completeness Verification", Scenario: "Task completion check"). Archive checks: "**WHEN** incomplete tasks are found (marked with `- [ ]`) **THEN** display all incomplete tasks to the user **AND** prompt for confirmation to continue **AND** default to 'No' for safety" (`OpenSpec/openspec/specs/cli-archive/spec.md`, Requirement: "Task Completion Check"). The plan is human-authored or agent-drafted prose; no estimation, dependency, autonomy-tier, or model-routing fields exist.

**What the manifesto requires.** `manifesto.md` defines: "**Plan** decomposes the design into executable steps." `manifesto-principles.md` P5 establishes that planning must select an autonomy tier with blast-radius justification, and P11 requires that economic routing (model tier per step) be planned before execution.

**The gap.** OpenSpec tasks.md has no autonomy-tier selection field, no blast-radius classification, no model-routing decision, no per-step risk class, and no link to the evaluation portfolio that will gate Verify. There is no required "non-decreasing coverage" or "rollback plan" entry. Tasks are prose and the only enforcement is "tasks checked off". Under EU AI Act Art. 14 (human oversight) and DORA Art. 5(2) (ICT governance — board-approved digital operational resilience strategy), the absence of an autonomy-tier and oversight-mode declaration in the plan is a control deficiency. Severity: **Medium**.

### Execute | **22/100**

**What OpenSpec does.** OpenSpec has a thin `/opsx:apply` action that drives an agent through tasks.md. `OpenSpec/docs/workflows.md` describes apply as: "`/opsx:apply` — Implement tasks — Ready to write code". There is no spec file under `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/` that defines tier-bound, sandboxed, or budget-constrained execution — `apply` is a Markdown skill instructing the agent to "work through tasks". The framework relies on the host coding agent (Claude/Codex/etc.) for any sandboxing; OpenSpec contributes no kill-switch, rate-limit, tool-manifest binding, or per-action authorisation. Telemetry (`OpenSpec/openspec/specs/telemetry/spec.md`, Requirement: "Privacy-preserving event design") deliberately strips all execution context: "The system SHALL NOT include command arguments, file paths, project names, spec content, error messages, or IP addresses in telemetry events."

**What the manifesto requires.** `manifesto.md` defines: "**Execute** carries out the plan within bounded autonomy." `manifesto-principles.md` P5 requires that execution operate inside a tiered autonomy budget with policy-enforced blast-radius caps; `manifesto.md` "What the Loop Produces" requires the execution to produce trace IDs linking the full decision chain.

**The gap.** OpenSpec contributes no execution bounding, no autonomy tier enforcement, no tool manifest, no per-action policy check, no trace-ID emission, no kill switch, and no per-action cost tracking. Telemetry is a deliberately blind opt-out probe with no link to the change being executed. Under DORA Art. 9 (ICT systems and tools), Art. 11 (response and recovery), EU AI Act Art. 12 (record-keeping — automatic logs over the lifetime of the system), and SR 11-7 §V (effective challenge — the agent's actions must be auditable as model output), this is a Critical deficiency for Allianz internal-model and underwriting agent use cases. Severity: **Critical**.

### Verify | **40/100**

**What OpenSpec does.** Verification is the `/opsx:verify` skill (`OpenSpec/openspec/specs/opsx-verify-skill/spec.md`). It produces a three-dimension report: Completeness (task counts and requirement-to-code search), Correctness (per-requirement codebase search and per-scenario coverage check), and Coherence (design.md adherence, code pattern consistency). The report ranks issues as `CRITICAL`, `WARNING`, `SUGGESTION` (Requirement: "Verification Report Format", Scenario: "Issue prioritization"). `OpenSpec/docs/workflows.md` line 277 states: "Verify won't block archive, but it surfaces issues you might want to address first." `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/opsx-verify-skill/spec.md` (Requirement: "Critical issues found") states: "**WHEN** CRITICAL issues exist **THEN** display: 'X critical issue(s) found. Fix before archiving.' **AND** do NOT suggest running archive" — but it does not technically prevent the user from running archive. Structural validation (`openspec validate --strict --json`) emits a stable schema `{ items[], summary, version }` (`OpenSpec/openspec/specs/cli-validate/spec.md`, Requirement: "Validation options and progress indication", Scenario: "JSON output schema for bulk validation") usable by CI to fail builds.

**What the manifesto requires.** `manifesto.md` defines: "**Verify** checks the output against the specification (did we build it right?)." `manifesto-done.md` requires for the **Verified** condition: "evaluated against regression tests (and adversarial cases), with an evidence bundle (diffs, trace IDs, policy check outputs) required for every automated merge."

**The gap.** OpenSpec's Verify is agent prose against agent prose: scenarios are not executable, regression and adversarial categories are not required, and no evidence bundle (trace IDs, policy check outputs, signed integrity hash) is assembled. `openspec validate --strict --json` checks Markdown shape only and is the sole CI-enforceable artefact. Under EU AI Act Art. 15 (accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity) and Solvency II Art. 124 (validation standards) the verification regime fails the "machine-checked correctness against the specification" bar. Severity: **High**.

### Validate | **18/100**

**What OpenSpec does.** OpenSpec collapses validation into the same `/opsx:verify` agent run that performs verification. `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/opsx-verify-skill/spec.md` (Requirement: "Verify Skill Invocation") instructs that "agent verifies implementation for that specific change" — i.e., the agent executing the implementation also produces the verification report. There is no separated "did we build the right thing?" gate. There is no organisationally separate validator role, no second-line check, no production-decision authority. `OpenSpec/docs/workflows.md` (`/opsx:verify` table line 273–275) describes Coherence as "Design decisions reflected in code, patterns consistent" — internal consistency, not external fitness for purpose.

**What the manifesto requires.** `manifesto.md` defines: "**Validate** checks the outcome against real-world need (did we build the right thing?). ... Verification and validation are distinct disciplines. Verification is technical correctness against the spec. Validation is fitness for intended use in the real world. An agent can pass every verification check and still fail validation. Both are required."

**The gap.** OpenSpec has no Validate phase. There is no required real-world-fitness check, no holdout case requirement, no field-evidence linkage, no organisationally separate validator. Under SR 11-7 §V (effective challenge), Solvency II Art. 48 (actuarial function) and Art. 124 (independent validation), and EU AI Act Art. 17 (quality management — distinct verification and validation procedures), this absence is a categorical compliance blocker for Allianz internal-model and underwriting agents. Severity: **Critical**.

### Observe | **14/100**

**What OpenSpec does.** OpenSpec's only runtime instrumentation is `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/telemetry/spec.md` Requirement: "Command execution tracking": "The system SHALL send a `command_executed` event to PostHog when any CLI command executes, including only the command name and OpenSpec version as properties." `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/telemetry/spec.md` Requirement: "Privacy-preserving event design" mandates: "The system SHALL NOT include command arguments, file paths, project names, spec content, error messages, or IP addresses in telemetry events." There is no spec coupling, no run ID, no agent identifier, no model identifier, no token cost, no policy-violation event, no drift signal. The framework has no observability of agent reasoning, of governance state, or of artefact freshness.

**What the manifesto requires.** `manifesto.md` defines: "**Observe** monitors runtime behavior, drift, and cost." `governance/integrated-audit-trail.md` AEM execution trace section requires that for every loop iteration, a trace records the decision chain (specification version, agent identifier, tool calls, model identifier, policy checks, outcome) so that reasoning can be reconstructed; `manifesto-principles.md` P9 establishes a governance-state observability minimum bar (override rates, waiver currency, control freshness).

**The gap.** OpenSpec does not produce or require any execution trace, decision chain, or governance-state telemetry. The product telemetry it does produce is deliberately decoupled from the change being executed. Under EU AI Act Art. 12 (record-keeping — automatic logs of events relevant to identifying risks and substantial modifications), DORA Art. 9 (ICT systems must enable continuous monitoring) and Art. 13 (learning and evolving — collection of information on incidents), and Solvency II Art. 41(3) (system of governance — written records of decisions), this is a Critical observability deficit for Allianz. Severity: **Critical**.

### Learn | **20/100**

**What OpenSpec does.** OpenSpec preserves change history by archiving each change folder under `openspec/changes/archive/YYYY-MM-DD-<change-name>/` (`OpenSpec/openspec/specs/cli-archive/spec.md`, Requirement: "Archive Process", Scenario: "Performing archive"). `OpenSpec/docs/concepts.md` Archive section states: "**Audit trail.** The archive preserves the full context of every change — not just what changed, but the proposal explaining why, the design explaining how, and the tasks showing the work done." Specs evolve through delta merge: `## ADDED`, `## MODIFIED`, `## REMOVED` operations rewrite `openspec/specs/<id>/spec.md` at archive time. There is no curated heuristic memory, no learned-routing memory, no incident-derived memory, no provenance for derived heuristics. The framework provides no mechanism to feed observed failures back into the next change beyond a human re-reading the archived prose.

**What the manifesto requires.** `manifesto.md` defines: "**Learn** updates knowledge and memory from observations. At Phases 4–5, this means: add durable findings to the knowledge base and curate learned memory with new heuristics, routing preferences, and reusable skills. ... Knowledge captures durable truth; memory captures learned heuristics and reusable skills."

**The gap.** OpenSpec has document archival, not learning infrastructure. There is no learned-memory artefact with provenance, no heuristic-update workflow, no routing-preference capture, no reusable-skill registry, and no failure-to-evaluation feedback. Under EU AI Act Art. 9(2)(c) (risk management — evaluation of risks emerging when in use) and DORA Art. 13 (learning and evolving from ICT incidents), the framework provides no learning loop. Severity: **High**.

### Govern | **15/100**

**What OpenSpec does.** OpenSpec has no governance phase. `openspec archive` (`OpenSpec/openspec/specs/cli-archive/spec.md`, Requirement: "Archive Process") moves the change to the archive directory after applying delta operations and prompts for confirmation only on incomplete tasks or spec-update side effects. `--yes` / `-y` (Requirement: "Confirmation Behavior") and `--skip-specs` (Requirement: "Skip Specs Option") allow bypass; `--no-validate` (Requirement: "Archive Validation", Scenario: "Force archive without validation") allows archive with validation skipped, displaying only "a warning about skipping validation". There is no policy violation register, no economics review, no accountability sign-off field, no waiver record, no expiry, no kill switch, no Tier-4 prerequisite check, no control state record. The accountable-human concept is absent from every spec under `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/`.

**What the manifesto requires.** `manifesto.md` defines: "**Govern** applies policy, accountability, change control, and economics review. ... A Govern cycle is not complete until: all outstanding policy violations are resolved, accountability signals are within threshold (no rubber-stamping pattern detected), economics review is recorded, any architectural decisions triggered by governance are filed back into Design, and tool invocations during the loop are confirmed within the authorized scope for the operating tier — any out-of-scope tool call is classified as a policy violation and triggers the remediation sub-cycle before the loop repeats." `governance/governance-integration-note.md` (AEM Tier 4 binary policy envelope) requires four operational prerequisites before any Tier 4 operation; `integration/low-consequence-resolution.md` confirms AEM has no consequence-class carve-out — every action carries the per-action accountability bar.

**The gap.** OpenSpec has none of the Govern completion conditions. No policy-violation tracking, no accountability signal threshold, no economics review, no architectural decision back-filing, no tool-scope confirmation, and no machine-readable control state record. Tier 4 prerequisites cannot even be modelled because there is no autonomy-tier construct. Under SR 11-7 §V, Solvency II Art. 41 (system of governance) and Art. 48 (actuarial function), DORA Art. 5 (ICT governance and control framework) and Art. 28 (general principles for ICT third-party risk management), and EU AI Act Art. 14 (human oversight) and Art. 17 (quality management system), this is a categorical compliance blocker for Allianz. Severity: **Critical**.

### Cross-Phase Failure Modes

- **Specify→Verify:** Loop-readiness gate is not enforced upstream and `/opsx:verify` cannot test it downstream — `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/cli-validate/spec.md` (Requirement: "Validation SHALL provide actionable remediation steps") accepts a proposal whose `## Why` is a sentence without measurable success criteria, and `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/opsx-verify-skill/spec.md` (Requirement: "Completeness Verification") only checks task completion and code search. EU AI Act Art. 9 (risk management system) is breached because risk-relevant scope is never recorded. Severity: Critical.
- **Plan→Execute:** Tasks.md does not declare an autonomy tier or a tool manifest, and `apply` has no enforcement layer — `OpenSpec/docs/concepts.md` (Tasks Artifact) defines tasks as prose checkboxes only and no spec under `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/` defines an `apply` enforcement contract. DORA Art. 9 (ICT systems, protocols and tools) is breached because tool invocation scope is unbounded. Severity: Critical.
- **Execute→Verify:** No execution trace exists to verify against — `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/telemetry/spec.md` (Requirement: "Privacy-preserving event design") strips any link between a command and the change, and `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/opsx-verify-skill/spec.md` (Requirement: "Correctness Verification") performs a fresh codebase search without trace evidence. EU AI Act Art. 12 (record-keeping) is breached. Severity: Critical.
- **Verify→Validate:** Verification and validation are folded into one agent skill — `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/opsx-verify-skill/spec.md` (Requirement: "Coherence Verification") is run by the same actor that produced the implementation, and `OpenSpec/docs/workflows.md` ("Verify Before Archiving") presents verify as the sole pre-archive check. SR 11-7 §V (effective challenge) is breached because no organisationally separate validator exists. Severity: Critical.
- **Verify→Govern:** Critical issues do not block archive — `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/opsx-verify-skill/spec.md` (Requirement: "Verification Report Format", Scenario: "Critical issues found") merely "does NOT suggest running archive", and `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/cli-archive/spec.md` (Requirement: "Skip Specs Option" plus `--no-validate` Scenario "Force archive without validation") allows archive even with structural validation skipped. Solvency II Art. 124 (validation standards) is breached because validation is non-blocking. Severity: Critical.
- **Observe→Learn:** Telemetry contains no signal usable for learning — `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/telemetry/spec.md` (Requirement: "Command execution tracking") restricts events to `{ command, version }`, and `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/cli-archive/spec.md` (Requirement: "Archive Process") performs a static folder move with no observation hand-off. DORA Art. 13 (learning and evolving) is breached. Severity: High.
- **Learn→Specify:** No mechanism feeds incident learnings into the next proposal — `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/cli-archive/spec.md` archives change folders with no derived heuristic, and `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/opsx-onboard-skill/spec.md` (Requirement: "Discovery and analysis") drafts new proposals without consulting archived failure data. EU AI Act Art. 9(2)(c) (risks emerging in use) is breached. Severity: High.
- **Govern→Design:** Architectural decisions triggered by governance are never filed back — there is no Govern phase artefact and `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/opsx-verify-skill/spec.md` (Requirement: "Coherence Verification", Scenario: "Design decision violated") only suggests "either update implementation or update design.md" as an advisory note. Solvency II Art. 41 (system of governance — written policies) is breached. Severity: High.

### Human Escalation Architecture

**Escalation triggers.** OpenSpec defines four trigger points, all of which are advisory or low-consequence: (1) interactive selection prompts in `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/cli-archive/spec.md` (Requirement: "Change Selection", Scenario: "Interactive selection") and `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/cli-validate/spec.md` (Requirement: "Top-level validate command", Scenario: "Interactive validation selection"); (2) incomplete-task confirmation in `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/cli-archive/spec.md` (Requirement: "Task Completion Check", Scenario: "Incomplete tasks found") that "default[s] to 'No' for safety"; (3) spec-update confirmation in the same file (Requirement: "Confirmation Behavior", Scenario: "Displaying confirmation"); (4) `/opsx:verify` CRITICAL display in `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/opsx-verify-skill/spec.md` (Requirement: "Verification Report Format", Scenario: "Critical issues found"), which "displays" but does not technically prevent archive. There is no escalation trigger for policy violation, autonomy-tier breach, drift detection, control-state failure, or accountability rubber-stamping.

**Escalation path.** No named role exists in any OpenSpec spec. The only actor referenced is "the user" running the CLI or "the agent" running the skill. There is no second-line role, no actuarial function, no risk officer, no Data Protection Officer, no model risk manager, no Tier 4 envelope owner. `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/cli-feedback/spec.md` (Requirement: "Feedback skill for agents") defines a `/feedback` skill that opens GitHub issues against the OpenSpec project itself — that is product feedback, not a user-organisation escalation channel. `governance/authority-accountability-matrix.md` AEM column has no counterpart in OpenSpec source files.

**Response time.** No response-time SLA is documented anywhere in `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/` or `OpenSpec/docs/`. The only timing guidance is `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/cli-validate/spec.md` (Requirement: "Validation options and progress indication", Scenario: "Concurrency limits for performance"), which sets validation parallelism (4–8) — an unrelated performance concern. There is no documented detection-to-intervention SLA, no waiver-expiry SLA, and no governance review cycle. `operational-templates/slo-table.md` feedback-loop closure SLOs and waiver-expiry SLOs have no counterpart artefact in OpenSpec.

**Fitness for Allianz context.** OpenSpec's escalation architecture is unfit for Allianz under at least the following named obligations from `domains/insurance.md`: (i) **Solvency II Art. 48** (actuarial function — requires a qualified actuarial reviewer with blocking authority for material model changes); (ii) **Solvency II Art. 41** (system of governance — written policies including escalation paths for risk events); (iii) **EU AI Act Art. 14** (human oversight — operational, capable of overriding the system, with documented competencies); (iv) **EU AI Act Art. 12** (record-keeping — automatic logs of events triggering human intervention); (v) **DORA Art. 5** (ICT governance and control framework — board-level accountability and documented escalation); (vi) **DORA Art. 17** (ICT-related incident management — classification, reporting, and escalation thresholds with response-time SLAs); (vii) **GDPR Art. 22** (HITL for solely automated decisions producing legal/significant effects on individuals — actual review, not theoretical availability); (viii) **SR 11-7 §V** (effective challenge — independent reviewer with blocking authority over deployment); (ix) **EIOPA AI Guidelines (2021)** (board-level AI accountability and second-line independent challenge — `domains/insurance.md` "EIOPA AI and ML Guidelines"); (x) **`domains/insurance.md` Hard Autonomy Caps** (Tier 1 ceilings for underwriting, claims adjudication, IDD-scope advisory, and SCR calculation — not enforceable in OpenSpec because no tier construct exists). The framework provides none of the required role definitions, SLAs, kill switches, or blocking gates. Severity: **Critical**.

#### Condition Narratives

**Shipped — 45 (High).** OpenSpec demonstrates a partial Shipped capability: `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/cli-archive/spec.md` Requirement: "Archive Process" specifies "Move the entire change directory to the archive location" with date-prefixed naming, and the spec-merge step rewrites `openspec/specs/<id>/spec.md` with the deltas. `manifesto-done.md` defines: "**Shipped** — deployed or delivered, not just merged." OpenSpec archives but does not deploy: there is no environment record, no production version reference, no deployment-time stamp, and no link to a release pipeline. The flag combinations `--yes --skip-specs --no-validate` allow archival of an unverified, unmerged change. Severity: **High**.

**Observable — 12 (Critical).** OpenSpec demonstrates no Observable capability beyond product analytics. `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/telemetry/spec.md` Requirement: "Privacy-preserving event design" mandates: "The system SHALL NOT include command arguments, file paths, project names, spec content, error messages, or IP addresses in telemetry events." `manifesto-done.md` defines: "**Observable** — instrumented and logged so reasoning can be inspected and reconstructed from traces." The telemetry by design is decoupled from any reasoning trace, and there is no separate trace, log, or audit-record artefact under `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/`. Severity: **Critical**.

**Verified — 32 (Critical).** OpenSpec demonstrates a Markdown-shape verifier and a prose verifier. `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/opsx-verify-skill/spec.md` Requirement: "Verification Report Format", Scenario: "Critical issues found" states: "**WHEN** CRITICAL issues exist **THEN** display: 'X critical issue(s) found. Fix before archiving.' **AND** do NOT suggest running archive". `manifesto-done.md` defines: "**Verified** — evaluated against regression tests (and adversarial cases), with an evidence bundle (diffs, trace IDs, policy check outputs) required for every automated merge." OpenSpec's verify produces neither regression coverage nor an evidence bundle; the bundle integrity attestation, security static analysis results, and agentic provenance record listed in `manifesto-done.md` have no counterpart. Severity: **Critical**.

**Provable — 8 (Critical).** OpenSpec demonstrates no Provable capability. `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/cli-validate/spec.md` Requirement: "Validation options and progress indication", Scenario: "JSON output schema for bulk validation" gives a deterministic JSON envelope but the input is Markdown shape, not invariants over behaviour. `manifesto-done.md` defines: "**Provable (when risk requires it)** — formalized invariants and replayable proof artifacts attached for critical workflows." OpenSpec has no invariant language, no replay harness, and no proof-artifact storage. For Allianz Solvency II internal-model agents the Provable bar is required and unmet. Severity: **Critical**.

**Learned from — 22 (High).** OpenSpec preserves changes via archival. `OpenSpec/docs/concepts.md` (Archive section) states: "**Audit trail.** The archive preserves the full context of every change". `manifesto-done.md` defines: "**Learned from** — knowledge base and learned memory updated with what was discovered, with provenance." OpenSpec preserves documents but does not curate knowledge or memory: no provenance-bound heuristics, no incident-derived learnings, no routing-preference updates. Severity: **High**.

**Governed — 8 (Critical).** OpenSpec demonstrates no Governed capability. `OpenSpec/openspec/specs/cli-archive/spec.md` Requirement: "Skip Specs Option", Scenario: "Skipping spec updates with flag" states: "**WHEN** executing `openspec archive <change> --skip-specs` **THEN** skip spec discovery and update confirmation **AND** proceed directly to moving the change to archive". `manifesto-done.md` defines: "**Governed** — operating within autonomy tiers appropriate to its risk, with human accountability assigned." OpenSpec has no autonomy tier, no accountable-human field, no policy register, and no control state record. Severity: **Critical**.

**Economical — 6 (Critical).** OpenSpec demonstrates no Economical capability. There is no cost telemetry beyond `command_executed`, no model-tier routing decision, and no cost-per-outcome attribution. `manifesto-done.md` defines: "**Economical** — routed through appropriate model tiers, cost tracked and justified per outcome." OpenSpec records neither model identity nor cost. Severity: **Critical**.

#### DoD Hardening Test

**Audit scenario.** A DORA Art. 17 ICT-related incident is reported on 2026-05-08 against an Allianz claims-triage agent product whose changes are tracked in OpenSpec. The supervisory authority requests, within seven business days, the evidence bundle for the most recent agent change deployed to production, including the agentic provenance record (`manifesto-done.md`), the security static analysis result, the rollback test result within the 48-hour pre-deployment window, the integrity attestation for the bundle, and the named accountable human's sign-off (`manifesto.md` "What the Loop Produces").

- **Shipped.** (a) Evidence: `openspec/changes/archive/2026-05-07-add-claims-triage-rule/` folder with proposal, design, tasks, and merged delta. (b) Missing: deployed environment identifier, production version, rollback procedure test record. (c) Verdict: **Partial**.
- **Observable.** (a) Evidence: PostHog `command_executed` events with `command` and `version`. (b) Missing: any execution trace, run ID, agent identifier, model identifier, policy-check log, or governance-state log linkable to the incident. (c) Verdict: **Fail**.
- **Verified.** (a) Evidence: structural validation JSON (`openspec validate --all --strict --json`), `/opsx:verify` Markdown report from the change author. (b) Missing: regression evaluation results, adversarial evaluation results, evidence bundle (diffs+trace IDs+policy outputs), bundle integrity attestation. (c) Verdict: **Fail**.
- **Provable.** (a) Evidence: none directly applicable. (b) Missing: replayable proof artifacts and formal invariants; OpenSpec has no construct for these. (c) Verdict: **Fail**.
- **Learned from.** (a) Evidence: archived prior changes available for human reading. (b) Missing: machine-readable knowledge or memory updates with provenance; no incident-to-evaluation linkage. (c) Verdict: **Fail**.
- **Governed.** (a) Evidence: archive confirmation prompts with default-No (when not bypassed). (b) Missing: accountable-human sign-off field, autonomy tier record, policy violation register, control state record, waiver expiry, kill-switch event log. (c) Verdict: **Fail**.
- **Economical.** (a) Evidence: none. (b) Missing: cost forecast, actual-vs-forecast divergence, model-tier routing record. (c) Verdict: **Fail**.

**Overall verdict:** **Fail**. Single largest evidence gap: the absence of an evidence bundle envelope binding execution traces, policy checks, agentic provenance, and an accountable-human sign-off to the change record — without it, six of the seven DoD conditions cannot be evidenced under DORA Art. 17 reporting timelines. Hardening is not complete.

#### Industry-Specific DoD Requirements

- **Shipped — Solvency II Art. 112–127 (internal model approval) and Art. 41 (system of governance).** Obligation: deployments of agent products that feed SCR calculation must be traceable to a supervisor-approved internal-model version with documented deployment record. Gap: OpenSpec archive records a folder move but no production version, environment, or supervisory approval reference. Consequence: Allianz cannot evidence that the deployed agent version corresponds to an approved internal-model state — a Solvency II compliance breach for Pillar 1 model use, and a reportable governance failure under Art. 41.
- **Observable — EU AI Act Art. 12 (record-keeping), DORA Art. 9 (continuous monitoring) and Art. 13 (learning and evolving), Solvency II Art. 41(3) (written records of decisions).** Obligation: automatic logs of events relevant to the system's lifecycle, drift, and incidents must be retained for the operational lifetime. Gap: OpenSpec telemetry deliberately strips all linking context and there is no execution trace, drift log, or governance-state log artefact. Consequence: in a DORA Art. 17 incident report, Allianz cannot reconstruct the agent's reasoning at the time of the incident; supervisory expectations under EIOPA AI Guidelines (2021) for explainability cannot be met.
- **Verified — EU AI Act Art. 15 (accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity), Solvency II Art. 124 (validation standards), DORA Art. 16/24 (digital operational resilience testing including TLPT), SR 11-7 §IV (model validation).** Obligation: regression, adversarial, and validation-by-independent-validator before production deployment, with a complete evidence bundle. Gap: OpenSpec `/opsx:verify` is non-blocking and prose-only; no adversarial scenario category exists; no evidence bundle is assembled. - **Provable — EU AI Act Art. 17 (quality management system) and Art. 9 (risk management — proven mitigations for high-risk systems).** Obligation: for high-risk AI systems, formalised invariants and replayable artifacts where risk requires it (e.g., fairness invariants for pricing, denial-rate invariants for underwriting). Gap: OpenSpec has no invariant language and no replay harness. Consequence: Allianz cannot demonstrate proof-grade evidence for Annex III §5(b) (insurance pricing/risk-assessment) high-risk obligations.
- **Learned from — DORA Art. 13 (learning and evolving from ICT-related incidents), EU AI Act Art. 9(2)(c) (risks emerging when in use).** Obligation: incident learnings must update risk assessments, evaluations, and operational controls. Gap: OpenSpec records no cost or model-tier identity. Consequence: Allianz cannot evidence proportionality or detect cost drift attributable to model-routing changes for OpenSpec-tracked agent changes.
